\section*{Supplementary Methods}

All analyses below use the same dual-reservoir pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic
model, treatment protocol, and baseline parameters as the main text
(vancomycin for 4~days, followed by linezolid for
42~days, 21~days of pre-treatment equilibration and 14~days of post-treatment
follow-up; total simulated course 81~days). The limit of detection (LOD) is
10~CFU/mL in both compartments. Unless otherwise noted, growth rates are held
at their calibrated baseline values ($\rho_S = 0.72~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R = 0.65~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, $\rho_S^r = 0.19~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R^r = 0.18~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, a 10\% reservoir growth-rate advantage
for the sensitive strain, mirroring the blood-compartment fitness cost), with
linezolid's reservoir tissue-penetration fraction lowered to
$0.30$ (from $0.45$) to compensate, since the reservoir linezolid-kill rate
scales with $\rho_S^r$ and would otherwise clear the reservoir entirely.
Linezolid's half-maximal concentration takes a single project-wide value,
$EC_{50,L} = 0.6~\mathrm{mg/L}$, used both as the fixed baseline in the
$E_{max,L}$ and $k_{immune}$ sweeps and as the sampling mean in the
$EC_{50,L}$ sweep. $\rho_S$ was raised from $0.61$ to
$0.72~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ (with $\rho_R$ rescaled to preserve the $\sim$10\%
fitness cost) when $EC_{50,L}$ was consolidated onto that single value:
the reservoir kill rate scales as
$E_{max,L}^{r} = E_{max,L}\,(\rho_S^r/\rho_S)$, so a higher $\rho_S$
weakens reservoir killing and restores the resistant reservoir to the
escape side of its switch, which the more potent $EC_{50,L}$ would
otherwise have eliminated.
Only the single parameter under study is varied in each sweep below. Escape
thresholds are located by bisection
(\texttt{scipy.optimize.brentq}) on the difference between the final
simulated compartment count and the LOD, rather than read off a coarse grid,
so each reported boundary is precise to the stated tolerance.

\section{Monte Carlo escape switch in linezolid potency ($EC_{50,L}$)}
\label{sec:s2}

\Cref{fig:mc_ec50_switch} places the $n = 1000$ Monte Carlo patient
population sampled from $EC_{50,L} \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}$
(mean $0.6~\mathrm{mg/L}$, $\mathrm{CV} = 1.1$) against the continuous
deterministic switch curve shown in the same figure's top panel. The top
panel shows final $R_b$/$R_{res}$ burden along a 131-point deterministic
sweep of $EC_{50,L} \in [0.05, 3.3]~\mathrm{mg/L}$, with the bisected
threshold ($0.49~\mathrm{mg/L}$) marked. The bottom panel shows where the
sampled patient population falls relative to that switch, each patient
colored by their individual simulated outcome (escape vs.\ suppressed).

\paragraph{Determination of the threshold.}
Escape is scored on the terminal state of each simulation rather than on any
transient feature of the trajectory. Writing
$R_{res}(t_{\mathrm{end}};\,EC_{50,L})$ for the resistant reservoir density
at the end of follow-up ($t_{\mathrm{end}} = 1944~\mathrm{h}$), obtained by
integrating the model from the fixed initial condition
$(S_b, R_b, S_{res}, R_{res}) = (0,\,0,\,100,\,100)$ with every parameter
other than $EC_{50,L}$ held at baseline, the switch is the root
$EC_{50,L}^{*}$ of
\begin{equation}
  f(EC_{50,L}) \;=\; R_{res}\!\left(t_{\mathrm{end}};\,EC_{50,L}\right)
  - \mathrm{LOD},
  \qquad f\!\left(EC_{50,L}^{*}\right) = 0 .
  \label{eq:ec50_threshold}
\end{equation}
Because a larger $EC_{50,L}$ denotes \emph{weaker} potency---a higher drug
concentration being required for the same effect---values above
$EC_{50,L}^{*}$ give $f > 0$ and are classified as escape, and values below
it give $f < 0$ and are classified as suppression. The criterion is thus an
explicit quantitative one, a detectable resistant burden at the end of
follow-up, and not a visual reading of the simulated time courses.

The root is located by Brent's method (\texttt{scipy.optimize.brentq}),
bracketed by the endpoints of the swept interval and solved to an absolute
tolerance of $10^{-3}~\mathrm{mg/L}$. Each evaluation of $f$ entails one
complete integration over the 81-day protocol (LSODA, relative and absolute
tolerances $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-9}$), several orders of magnitude tighter
than the LOD-scale quantities compared in \Cref{eq:ec50_threshold}, so the
located boundary reflects model dynamics rather than integration error. The
sign condition $f(0.05) < 0 < f(3.3)$ is verified before bisection; its
failure would indicate that no crossing exists within the swept interval,
and is reported as an absent threshold rather than propagating silently as
an incorrect value. The 131-point grid serves as a diagnostic---it shows the
transition to be a single sharp monotone switch rather than a gradual
decline, supporting the uniqueness of the root that bisection returns---and
supplies the plotted curve, but it does not set the reported value, which is
correspondingly independent of the grid spacing.

Applying the same procedure to the blood compartment gives a crossing that
agrees with the reservoir's $0.49~\mathrm{mg/L}$ to within the bisection
tolerance and is therefore not resolved as a separate value. The two are in
any case expected to coincide, since both compartments experience the same
linezolid effect term and are coupled by bidirectional bacterial exchange. A
single threshold of $0.49~\mathrm{mg/L}$ is therefore reported for both.
Thresholds throughout this supplement are quoted to two significant
figures; the root-find itself is converged to
$10^{-3}~\mathrm{mg/L}$, so no significance is attached to differences at
or below that scale. The reservoir value is
adopted as the operative threshold throughout, since it is the reservoir that
seeds post-treatment relapse.

The Monte Carlo escape fraction reported in the main text is driven by this
single deterministic boundary.
The escape fractions represent how close the calibration landed to
its own threshold. The model is not comfortably clear of
relapse along any of the three dimensions examined (drug-potency
parameter, drug-efficacy parameter, and a host-immunity parameter).
It sits close to criticality in all three, independently and
simultaneously. The question of whether that reflects an
incidental property of this particular calibration, or a more general
feature of relapsing reservoir infections that have already reached the
point of requiring a change in antibiotics, linezolid in this particular
instance, is unclear. The question of patients who are near-critical across
multiple physiological axes at once is one this model cannot resolve. It
may be that the small mean-to-threshold gaps represent critically ill
patients whose ultimate outcome cannot be precisely determined.

\section{Cross-parameter consistency of the Monte Carlo escape thresholds}
\label{sec:s6b}

The three Monte Carlo sweeps above ($EC_{50,L}$,
$E_{max,L}$, $k_{immune}$) each report different
escape (clinical relapse) fractions of the sampled populations: $44\%$,
$24\%$ and $26\%$ respectively.
This section checks whether those fractions are a coincidence of the three
reported percentages or a consequence of where each calibrated parameter
sits relative to its own escape threshold.

Each parameter is sampled as $X \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\mu_{\ln},
\sigma_{\ln})$, parametrized by a linear-scale mean and coefficient of
variation ($\mathrm{CV} = \mathrm{SD}/\mathrm{mean}$) via
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\ln} = \sqrt{\ln\!\left(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2\right)}, \qquad
    \mu_{\ln} = \ln(\mathrm{mean}) - \frac{\sigma_{\ln}^2}{2},
    \label{eq:lognormal_params}
\end{equation}
the second term in $\mu_{\ln}$ ensuring $E[X] = \mathrm{mean}$. The
distance between the distribution mean and the independently bisected escape
threshold, expressed in standard deviations of the underlying normal
variable $\ln X$, is
\begin{equation}
    z = \frac{\ln(\text{threshold}) - \mu_{\ln}}{\sigma_{\ln}},
    \label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
and the predicted escape fraction is the tail probability of the standard
normal cumulative distribution function (CDF),
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(z) = \frac{1}{2}\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
evaluated on the side of $z$ that corresponds to escape for that
parameter. The side that represents escape depends on the parameter being measured.  Higher values of $EC_{50,L}$ correspond to
\emph{weaker} drug effect, which corresponds to 
escape in the \emph{upper} tail, $P(Z>z) = 1-\Phi(z)$.   A \emph{lower}
sampled $E_{max,L}$ or $k_{immune}$ means weaker killing capacity or weaker
immune clearance respectively. Escape for both is in the \emph{lower}
tail, $P(Z<z) = \Phi(z)$ of the normal distribution.

As a worked example, $EC_{50,L}$'s calibration (mean $=0.6$~mg/L,
$\mathrm{CV}=1.1$, threshold $=0.488$~mg/L; \Cref{sec:s2}) gives, from
\Cref{eq:lognormal_params}, $\sigma_{\ln} = \sqrt{\ln(1+1.1^2)} = 0.8905$
and $\mu_{\ln} = \ln(0.6) - 0.8905^2/2 = -0.9073$; substituting into
\Cref{eq:zscore} gives $z = \left[\ln(0.488) - (-0.9073)\right]/0.8905
\approx 0.213$. Since escape is the upper tail for $EC_{50,L}$, the
predicted escape fraction from \Cref{eq:phi} is $1-\Phi(0.213) =
1-0.584 = 0.416$, i.e.\ 42\%.  This value reported in the first row of
\Cref{tab:zscore_summary}. The other two rows follow the identical
procedure, substituting each parameter's own mean, CV, and threshold and
taking the lower tail instead of the upper. In practice $\Phi$ is evaluated
numerically (e.g.\ \texttt{scipy.stats.norm.cdf(z)}).  \Cref{eq:phi} is the exact quantity that is computed.

\Cref{tab:zscore_summary} applies \Cref{eq:lognormal_params,eq:zscore} to
the three calibrated sweeps. The $z$-score alone predicts each reported escape fraction to within a few percentage points. In every case the calibrated mean sits
under one standard deviation from its own threshold, and $EC_{50,L}$ is
now the closest of the three (0.21~SD) rather than the furthest, against
$k_{immune}$ and $E_{max,L}$ at 0.61--0.69~SD. Its sampling mean
($0.6$~mg/L) and its bisected threshold ($0.488$~mg/L) are separated by
about a fifth of a standard deviation of $\ln X$, which is why its escape
fraction sits near one half: a calibration that close to its own switch
splits the sampled population almost evenly.

\section{Scalar outcome sweeps for the compartment exchange rates}
\label{sec:s7}

\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes} summarizes Sweep~1 ($f_{r \to b}$ varied,
$f_{b \to r}$ held at baseline) in a single view: peak $R_b$ and final
$R_b$ overlaid on one log-scale axis, with the swept range shaded
by regime and the two precise, bisected thresholds marked. The two
thresholds answer different clinical questions and sit at opposite ends of
the seven-decade range. The establishment threshold
($f_{r \to b} = 2.2\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$) asks whether bacteremia
happens at all: below it, the reservoir-to-blood seeding flux is too small
for blood to ever accumulate enough bacteria to escape the model's
near-zero floor, so infection simply never establishes over the entire
81-day course. The clearance threshold
($f_{r \to b} = 8.3\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), roughly 370-fold
higher, asks a different question given that bacteremia does establish:
does it relapse after treatment, or resolve permanently? Below it ---
across the whole plateau in between --- resistant bacteremia establishes,
is suppressed by linezolid during therapy, and relapses afterward because
the reservoir sanctuary is never fully sterilized. Above it, transfer
becomes fast and one-directional enough that the reservoir stops
protecting the resistant strain: bacteria are funneled into blood faster
than they return, where they face far stronger immune killing and
linezolid's full, undiscounted effect, driving both compartments to
extinction together and leaving nothing behind to cause a relapse. In
short, the low threshold is about ignition --- can the reservoir seed
blood at all --- while the high threshold is about sanctuary --- can the
reservoir protect the resistant strain long enough to cause relapse; the
broad plateau between them is where the exact rate barely matters because
the outcome is already decided by which side of each threshold it falls
on.

Concretely, below the establishment threshold (bisected on the point where
final $R_b$ crosses the LOD) all three curves sit at or near zero, then
switch sharply to their full baseline levels within roughly half a
log-decade of the model's own baseline rate ($5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$).
This transition is a threshold across independent simulated courses ---
each grid point holds its own $f_{r \to b}$ fixed for the entire simulated
course --- rather than a switch occurring partway through any single
patient's timeline.

Across the broad plateau separating the two transitions ($2.2\times10^{-5}$
to $8.3\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, the salmon-shaded band in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes}), blood and reservoir diverge: once
established, $R_b$ is suppressed below the LOD for essentially the entire
linezolid course (at the baseline rate, $R_b$ falls from a pre-linezolid peak
of $\sim4.9\times10^3$~CFU/mL to $\sim0.03$~CFU/mL within days of linezolid
starting and stays there until the course ends), while $R_{res}$ is only
partially suppressed and never crosses the LOD, settling on a lower plateau
(down from a pre-treatment peak of $\sim9.3\times10^3$~CFU/mL) because
linezolid reaches the reservoir at only
$\mathrm{lzd\_res\_fraction} = 0.30$ of its blood exposure. That suppressed
level is not itself constant across the band: sampled at 40 log-spaced rates
strictly inside the plateau, $R_{res}$ at the end of the linezolid course
falls monotonically with $f_{r \to b}$, from $\sim6.0\times10^2$~CFU/mL just
above the establishment threshold ($\sim5.9\times10^2$ at the baseline rate
of $5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$) to $\sim4.5\times10^1$~CFU/mL just below
the clearance threshold --- a factor of 13 across the band, and within a
factor of $\sim4.2$ of the LOD at its upper end. The reservoir is therefore
progressively drained as $f_{r \to b}$ rises, well before the clearance
threshold is reached; the sanctuary weakens continuously rather than
surviving intact until it fails abruptly. Once linezolid is
withdrawn, this uncleared reservoir reseeds blood through $f_{r \to b}$, and
$R_b$ rebounds within days to a level comparable to its pre-treatment peak.
Final $R_b$ therefore closely tracks peak $R_b$ not because the resistant
population sits at carrying capacity in blood throughout treatment, but
because the post-treatment relapse reaches the same order of magnitude as the
pre-treatment escape: linezolid clears the resistant strain from blood but
never eradicates it from its reservoir sanctuary, for essentially the entire
escape range.

\Cref{tab:frb_plateau_relapse} makes this within-course sequence explicit at
seven rates spanning the plateau. Two features are worth noting. First, the
relapse is complete rather than partial: final $R_b$ matches peak $R_b$ to
within $0.1\%$ at every rate sampled, so the post-treatment
rebound recovers the full pre-treatment burden and not some fraction of it.
Second, the relapse is strictly a withdrawal phenomenon --- $R_b$ stays
between $\sim2\times10^{-2}$ and $\sim1.5$~CFU/mL for the entire 42-day linezolid
course, two to three orders of magnitude below the LOD, and only crosses
back above it 0.6--3.0~days \textit{after} the course ends. Nothing
resembling relapse occurs while drug is on board. The rebound lag shortens
as $f_{r \to b}$ increases, for the same reason $R_{res}$ at end-of-course
falls: faster reservoir-to-blood transfer both drains the sanctuary during
treatment and reseeds blood more rapidly once treatment stops.

At the upper end of the range (the medium seagreen-shaded band in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes}), the difference between final $R_b$ and peak $R_b$ represent the effect of time.
Peak $R_b$ and final $R_b$ are read at
different points in the course, and beyond the clearance threshold those
points fall on opposite sides of treatment. Inside the plateau the maximum
occurs after the linezolid course ends because the maximum
is the post-withdrawal relapse, which is why peak and final coincide
there (\Cref{tab:frb_plateau_relapse}). Beyond the clearance threshold no
relapse occurs, so the maximum reverts to the pre-treatment establishment
peak when linezolid begins. 

That reading holds only up to $f_{r \to b} \approx 9.2\times10^{-2}
~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, where peak $R_b$ itself steps down --- visibly, in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes} --- from $\sim5.5\times10^{3}$ to
$\sim1.8\times10^{3}$~CFU/mL across a rate change of under $5\%$, with the
time of the peak jumping from day $22.8$ to day $0.7$. This is a third
transition, and it is competitive rather than pharmacological: it occurs
before any drug is given. Efflux to blood enters both reservoir equations
as a loss term $-f_{r \to b}X_{res}$, while the two strains share a single
logistic ceiling, $1 - (S_{res}+R_{res})/B_{max,res}$. Raising
$f_{r \to b}$ therefore lowers the reservoir density at which each strain
breaks even, and the resistant strain --- growing more slowly there
($\rho_R^r = 0.1765$ against $\rho_S^r = 0.19415~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$) ---
reaches its break-even point first. Past that rate the sensitive strain's
own equilibrium density exceeds the level at which the resistant strain
can still hold its share, and $R_{res}$ is competitively excluded from the
reservoir while $S_{res}$ persists: at day $21$, the last day before
vancomycin, $R_{res}$ falls from $2.6\times10^{2}$~CFU/mL at
$f_{r \to b} = 5\times10^{-2}$ to $2.9\times10^{-2}$ at $10^{-1}$, while
$S_{res}$ over the same interval only declines from $5.5\times10^{3}$ to
$3.5\times10^{3}$. Deprived of its reservoir source and outgrown in blood
($\rho_S = 0.72$ against $\rho_R = 0.649~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), $R_b$ follows:
at day $21$ it falls from $2.1\times10^{2}$~CFU/mL to below the LOD at
$9.2\times10^{-2}$ and to $\sim10^{-4}$~CFU/mL beyond it. The residual
$\sim1.8\times10^{3}$ peak is then only a startup transient at day
$0.7$, as the initial $100$~CFU/mL reservoir inoculum washes into blood
before $S_b$ overtakes it. Above this rate the resistant strain therefore
never establishes at all, rather than establishing and being cured.

Unlike the plateau regime, this second transition
\textit{is} a genuine within-course dynamic: $R_b$ rises to near carrying
capacity during the outbreak and is then driven back down by linezolid and
the immune response before follow-up ends --- and $R_{res}$ collapses along
with it. Because $f_{b \to r}$ is held at its baseline value while $f_{r \to
b}$ climbs a further two decades, transfer becomes strongly
one-directional: reservoir bacteria are funneled into blood, where they are
exposed to immune killing an order of magnitude stronger than in the
reservoir, and to linezolid's full, undiscounted effect (rather than the
reservoir's tissue-penetration-scaled fraction), faster than they return. At
sufficiently high $f_{r \to b}$, the reservoir therefore stops acting as a
protected sanctuary and instead feeds the resistant population into a
compartment where it is cleared, driving both $R_b$ and $R_{res}$ to
extinction together.

\section{Sampling grid for the compartment exchange-rate sweeps}
\label{sec:s5}

The two exchange-rate sweeps in \Cref{sec:s7}
($f_{r \to b}$, reservoir $\to$ blood; $f_{b \to r}$, blood $\to$ reservoir)
each step their target rate across a shared log-spaced grid spanning seven
decades ($10^{-7}$--$10^{0}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), while holding the other
exchange rate fixed at its own baseline value
($f_{r \to b} = 5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$f_{b \to r} = 1\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$). $f_{r \to b}$'s transition
band, where established resistant bacteremia switches from relapsing to
clearing outright, falls within $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$
(\Cref{sec:s7}); $f_{b \to r}$ produces no comparable switch anywhere in the
swept range, but its steepest response --- the monotonic rise in peak
$S_{res}$ --- is also concentrated in that same band. The grid is therefore
deliberately non-uniform for both sweeps: a coarse 40-point log-spaced pass
covers the full seven-decade range for context, supplemented by two dense
passes --- 160 points across $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ and a
further 120 across $[10^{-2}, 10^{0}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, the latter added to
resolve the competitive-exclusion step at $f_{r \to b} \approx
9.2\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, where peak $R_b$ drops threefold
across a rate change of under $5\%$ (\Cref{sec:s7}); the coarse pass
alone places only about six points across those last $1.5$ decades and
renders the step as a single straight segment --- so that
$f_{r \to b}$'s sharp switch and the steepest portion of $f_{b \to r}$'s
smooth $S_{res}$ response (both visible in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes,fig:supp_fbr_regimes}) are both resolved rather
than aliased.
\Cref{fig:transfer_rate_grid} shows the resulting grid coverage for both
sweeps directly, with each sweep's own baseline rate marked.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{figures/transfer_rate_sweep_grid.png}
    \caption{Log-spaced grid coverage used for the two exchange-rate sweeps
    reported in the main text ($n = 193$ points per sweep). Top: grid for
    Sweep~1 ($f_{r \to b}$ varied, $f_{b \to r}$ held at baseline). Bottom:
    grid for Sweep~2 ($f_{b \to r}$ varied, $f_{r \to b}$ held at baseline).
    Both sweeps use the same shared grid of 302 points, concentrating 160
    inside $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ --- the band containing
    $f_{r \to b}$'s relapse-versus-clearance transition (\Cref{sec:s7}) ---
    and a further 119 inside $[10^{-2}, 10^{0}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, which
    covers the reservoir collapse, with the remaining 23 points spread
    log-uniformly across the full seven-decade range for context. The band's boundaries are not arbitrary:
    $1/(10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}) \approx 42$~days and
    $1/(10^{-2}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}) \approx 4$~days match the linezolid
    (42-day) and vancomycin (4-day) course lengths almost exactly, so this
    band brackets the regime where $f_{r \to b}$'s exchange timescale is
    comparable to a treatment-phase duration -- below
    $10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ exchange is too slow to complete even once
    during the linezolid course, and above $10^{-2}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ the
    compartments equilibrate within the (shorter) vancomycin window, so
    $f_{r \to b}$'s outcome saturates on either side. $f_{b \to r}$ occupies
    the identical timescale band without producing a comparable switch
    (\Cref{sec:s7}): it governs a reversible efflux out of blood rather than
    a seeding route into it, so matching a treatment-phase timescale is not
    by itself sufficient to gate the outcome.}

\end{figure}

\section{Deterministic escape threshold in initial reservoir burden ($S_{res,0}$)}
\label{sec:s3}

\Cref{fig:sres0_threshold_sweep} sweeps the initial reservoir sensitive-strain
load $S_{res,0} \in [1, 2000]~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$ (101 points), holding
$R_{res,0} = 100~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$ and every other parameter at baseline, to
test how sensitive the escape outcome is to the size of the pre-treatment
reservoir population rather than to any pharmacodynamic parameter. Unlike the
$EC_{50,L}$ sweep in \Cref{sec:s2}, no clearance threshold exists anywhere in
the swept range: both $R_b$ and $R_{res}$ finish above the LOD at every one
of the 101 tested $S_{res,0}$ values, from 1 to 2000~CFU/mL. The model's own
default, $S_{res,0} = 100~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$, is marked directly on the panel
for reference, but is not distinguished from any other tested value in its
outcome. Resistant escape in this model is therefore not an artifact of the
specific initial reservoir burden assumed in the main text --- it persists
regardless of where $S_{res,0}$ is set within a two-decade range around that
default.
