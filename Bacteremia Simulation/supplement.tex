\section*{Supplementary Methods}

All analyses below use the same dual-reservoir pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic
model, treatment protocol, and baseline parameters as the main text
(vancomycin 1000~mg q8h for 4~days, followed by linezolid 600~mg q12h for
42~days, 21~days of pre-treatment equilibration and 14~days of post-treatment
follow-up; total simulated course 81~days). The limit of detection (LOD) is
10~CFU/mL in both compartments. Unless otherwise noted, growth rates are held
at their calibrated baseline values ($\rho_S = 0.61~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R = 0.55~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, $\rho_S^r = 0.175~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R^r = 0.1765~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), and only the single parameter under
study is varied. Escape thresholds are located by bisection
(\texttt{scipy.optimize.brentq}) on the difference between the final
simulated compartment count and the LOD, rather than read off a coarse grid,
so each reported boundary is precise to the stated tolerance.

\section{Scalar outcome sweeps for the compartment exchange rates}
\label{sec:s4}

\Cref{fig:supp_frb_outcomes,fig:supp_frb_outcomes_zoom} give the full-range
and zoomed scalar-outcome views for Sweep~1 ($f_{r \to b}$ varied, $f_{b \to
r}$ held at baseline). Peak $S_b$, peak $R_b$, and final $R_b$ all sit at or
near zero for $f_{r \to b} \lesssim 3\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ --- blood
is seeded exclusively through reservoir-to-blood transfer, so vanishingly
small $f_{r \to b}$ leaves bacteremia unestablished for the entire simulated
course --- then switch sharply to their full baseline levels within roughly
half a log-decade of the model's own baseline rate
($5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$). This transition is a threshold across
independent simulated courses --- each grid point holds its own $f_{r \to
b}$ fixed for the entire simulated course --- rather than a switch occurring
partway through any single patient's timeline. Peak $S_{res}$ is flat
across the full six-decade range.

Across the broad plateau separating the two transitions ($\sim3\times10^{-5}$
to $\sim5\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), $R_b$ that reaches carrying
capacity stays there: final $R_b$ closely tracks peak $R_b$, so once
resistant bacteremia establishes it persists through the remainder of
linezolid therapy and relapses to the same level after treatment ends, for
essentially the entire escape range.

At the upper end of the range, final $R_b$ undergoes a second, independent
transition, dropping back to zero for $f_{r \to b} \gtrsim
5\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ even as peak $R_b$ continues to rise ---
$R_b$ still establishes and peaks at these rates, but no longer relapses by
the end of follow-up. Unlike the plateau regime, this second transition
\textit{is} a genuine within-course dynamic: $R_b$ rises to near carrying
capacity during the outbreak and is then driven back down by linezolid and
the immune response before follow-up ends --- and $R_{res}$ collapses along
with it. Because $f_{b \to r}$ is held at its baseline value while $f_{r \to
b}$ climbs a further two decades, transfer becomes strongly
one-directional: reservoir bacteria are funneled into blood, where they are
exposed to immune killing an order of magnitude stronger than in the
reservoir, and to linezolid's full, undiscounted effect (rather than the
reservoir's tissue-penetration-scaled fraction), faster than they return. At
sufficiently high $f_{r \to b}$, the reservoir therefore stops acting as a
protected sanctuary and instead feeds the resistant population into a
compartment where it is cleared, driving both $R_b$ and $R_{res}$ to
extinction together.

\section{Sampling grid for the compartment exchange-rate sweeps}
\label{sec:s5}

The two exchange-rate sweeps in \Cref{sec:s4}
($f_{r \to b}$, reservoir $\to$ blood; $f_{b \to r}$, blood $\to$ reservoir)
each step their target rate across a shared log-spaced grid spanning six
decades ($10^{-8}$--$10^{-2}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), while holding the other
exchange rate fixed at its own baseline value
($f_{r \to b} = 5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$f_{b \to r} = 1\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$). Because both transition bands
identified in the main text fall within $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
the grid is deliberately non-uniform: a coarse 40-point log-spaced pass covers
the full six-decade range for context, supplemented by a dense 160-point pass
restricted to that transition band, so that the sharp switches reported in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_outcomes_zoom,fig:supp_fbr_outcomes_zoom} are resolved
rather than aliased. \Cref{fig:transfer_rate_grid} shows the resulting grid
coverage for both sweeps directly, with each sweep's own baseline rate
marked.

\section{Base model: dual-reservoir PK/PD structure}
\label{sec:s10}

All analyses above share a single underlying model, implemented in
\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py}. It has three parts: (1) a two-drug
pharmacokinetic (PK) model that converts a fixed dosing regimen into a
free-drug concentration function of time, (2) a fixed-rate host immune
response, and (3) a four-compartment pharmacodynamic (PD) ODE system,
\texttt{dual\_reservoir\_model}, coupling a blood compartment and a
reservoir (deep-tissue/endocardial vegetation) compartment, each split into
a vancomycin/linezolid-sensitive strain and a linezolid-only-susceptible
resistant strain.

\subsection{Pharmacokinetic model}

\texttt{PharmacokineticModel} (\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py:11-57}) is a
one-compartment model with fixed linear clearance for each drug --- repeated
IV bolus dosing with first-order elimination, no absorption phase. For a
drug with elimination rate $k_e = CL/V_d$, dose $D$, dosing interval
$\tau$, and free fraction $f_u$, the free-drug concentration from dose $n$
(given at $t_n = t_{start} + n\tau$) is

\begin{equation}
C(t) = f_u \sum_{n\,:\,t_n \le t} \frac{D}{V_d}\, e^{-k_e (t - t_n)},
\qquad t_n \in [t_{start},\, t_{start}+\text{duration}),
\end{equation}

implemented numerically as a superposition of exponential decay tails on a
dense time grid (10 points/hour) and returned as an interpolating function
\texttt{conc\_func(t)}. Concentration is clamped to zero before
\texttt{start\_h}. Two drugs are parameterized (\Cref{tab:pk}); both use
the same functional form, called once each to build \texttt{van\_func(t)}
and \texttt{lzd\_func(t)}, which are then passed unchanged into the ODE
system as forcing functions of time.

\subsection{Immune response}

\texttt{ImmuneResponse} (\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py:60-79}) is a constant
(time-independent) host clearance rate, $k_{immune}$, scaled independently
per compartment by two multipliers: $\mathrm{eff}_{blood}$ (default $1.0$)
and $\mathrm{eff}_{res}$ (default $0.1$). The reservoir compartment is
therefore, by construction, an order of magnitude harder for the immune
system to clear than blood --- representing reduced immune access to a
deep-seated/vegetative focus of infection. A code comment at
\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py:64-73} records that a strain-specific (rather
than compartment-specific) immune multiplier was tried and rejected: it
produced a bimodal switch (resistant blood infection either never appears or
relapses to $\gtrsim4\times10^3$ CFU/mL) with no stable intermediate
behavior, so the model reverted to a single shared $k_{immune}$ per
compartment.

\subsection{Pharmacodynamics}

Both drugs act through saturating $E_{max}$ (Hill, $h=1$) kill/inhibition
terms:

\begin{equation}
E_V(t) = \frac{E_{max,V}\, V(t)}{EC_{50,V} + V(t)}, \qquad
E_L(t) = \frac{E_{max,L}\, L(t)}{EC_{50,L} + L(t)},
\end{equation}

where $V(t)$, $L(t)$ are the free vancomycin/linezolid concentrations from
the PK model above. Vancomycin is modeled as \textbf{bactericidal} ---
$E_V$ enters the sensitive-strain equations as an added death-rate term.
Linezolid is modeled as \textbf{bacteriostatic} --- $E_L$ enters as a
death-rate term for \emph{both} strains (it is the resistant strain's only
source of drug susceptibility in this model; $R_b$/$R_{res}$ have no
vancomycin term at all). Reservoir exposure to each drug is discounted
relative to blood by fixed tissue-penetration fractions,
\texttt{van\_res\_fraction} (default $0.15$) and \texttt{lzd\_res\_fraction}
(default $0.45$), reflecting reduced drug penetration into a
vegetation/deep-tissue focus; the reservoir's linezolid $E_{max}$ is
additionally rescaled by the ratio of reservoir to blood sensitive-strain
growth rates, $E_{max,L}^{res} = E_{max,L} \cdot \rho_{res,S}/\rho_S$
(\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py:161}).

\subsection{Compartment structure and the ODE system}

\texttt{dual\_reservoir\_model} (\texttt{model\_Bacteremia.py:83-184}) tracks
four state variables: $S_b$/$R_b$ (sensitive/resistant strain, blood) and
$S_{res}$/$R_{res}$ (sensitive/resistant strain, reservoir). Each population
grows logistically toward a compartment-specific carrying capacity
($B_{max,blood}$, shared by $S_b+R_b$; $B_{max,reservoir}$, shared by
$S_{res}+R_{res}$), is killed by the immune response and, where susceptible,
by drug, and exchanges with its counterpart compartment at fixed
first-order rates $f_{r \to b}$ (reservoir $\to$ blood) and $f_{b \to r}$
(blood $\to$ reservoir) --- the same exchange rates swept in
\Cref{sec:s4,sec:s5}. A smooth low-density floor, $x^2/(10^2+x^2)$,
multiplies each growth term so that populations run down to true extinction
rather than stalling at a small positive value as they approach zero, and
also prevents solver stiffness there. The full system:

\begin{align}
\dot S_b &= \rho_S S_b \Big(1-\tfrac{S_b+R_b}{B_{max,blood}}\Big)
\tfrac{S_b^2}{10^2+S_b^2} - E_L S_b - \mathrm{eff}_{blood}\,k_{immune}\,S_b
- E_V S_b + f_{r\to b} S_{res} - f_{b\to r} S_b \\[2pt]
\dot R_b &= \rho_R R_b \Big(1-\tfrac{S_b+R_b}{B_{max,blood}}\Big)
\tfrac{R_b^2}{10^2+R_b^2} - E_L R_b - \mathrm{eff}_{blood}\,k_{immune}\,R_b
+ f_{r\to b} R_{res} - f_{b\to r} R_b \\[2pt]
\dot S_{res} &= \rho_{res,S} S_{res} \Big(1-\tfrac{S_{res}+R_{res}}{B_{max,res}}\Big)
\tfrac{S_{res}^2}{10^2+S_{res}^2} - E_L^{res} S_{res} - \mathrm{eff}_{res}\,k_{immune}\,S_{res}
- E_V^{res} S_{res} - f_{r\to b} S_{res} + f_{b\to r} S_b \\[2pt]
\dot R_{res} &= \rho_{res,R} R_{res} \Big(1-\tfrac{S_{res}+R_{res}}{B_{max,res}}\Big)
\tfrac{R_{res}^2}{10^2+R_{res}^2} - E_L^{res} R_{res} - \mathrm{eff}_{res}\,k_{immune}\,R_{res}
- f_{r\to b} R_{res} + f_{b\to r} R_b
\end{align}

Note the asymmetry that drives the model's central resistance narrative:
$R_b$ and $R_{res}$ have no vancomycin term at all (resistant to
vancomycin by construction), while $S_b$/$S_{res}$ are killed by both drugs.
$R_{res}$'s only loss terms are linezolid, the (weak) reservoir immune
response, and net outward exchange --- so wherever $\rho_{res,R}$ exceeds
those combined losses, $R_{res}$ persists in the reservoir independent of
treatment and can continually reseed $R_b$ in blood through $f_{r\to b}$.

parameterization in which the untreated host would clear infection on its
