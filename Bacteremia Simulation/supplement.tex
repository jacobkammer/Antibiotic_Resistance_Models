\section*{Supplementary Methods}

All analyses below use the same dual-reservoir pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic
model, treatment protocol, and baseline parameters as the main text
(vancomycin for 4~days, followed by linezolid for
42~days, 21~days of pre-treatment equilibration and 14~days of post-treatment
follow-up; total simulated course 81~days). The limit of detection (LOD) is
10~CFU/mL in both compartments. Unless otherwise noted, growth rates are held
at their calibrated baseline values ($\rho_S = 0.61~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R = 0.55~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, $\rho_S^r = 0.19415~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$\rho_R^r = 0.1765~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, a 10\% reservoir growth-rate advantage
for the sensitive strain, mirroring the blood-compartment fitness cost), with
linezolid's reservoir tissue-penetration fraction lowered to
$0.30$ (from $0.45$) to compensate, since the reservoir linezolid-kill rate
scales with $\rho_S^r$ and would otherwise clear the reservoir entirely.
Only the single parameter under study is varied in each sweep below. Escape
thresholds are located by bisection
(\texttt{scipy.optimize.brentq}) on the difference between the final
simulated compartment count and the LOD, rather than read off a coarse grid,
so each reported boundary is precise to the stated tolerance.

\section{Cross-parameter consistency of the Monte Carlo escape thresholds}
\label{sec:s6b}

The three Monte Carlo sweeps above ($EC_{50,L}$, \Cref{sec:s2};
$E_{max,L}$, \Cref{sec:s3}; $k_{immune}$, \Cref{sec:s6}) each report an
escape (relapse) fraction close to one quarter of the sampled population.
This section checks whether that clustering is a coincidence of the three
reported percentages or a consequence of where each calibrated parameter
sits relative to its own escape threshold.

Each parameter is sampled as $X \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\mu_{\ln},
\sigma_{\ln})$, parametrized by a linear-scale mean and coefficient of
variation ($\mathrm{CV} = \mathrm{SD}/\mathrm{mean}$) via
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\ln} = \sqrt{\ln\!\left(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2\right)}, \qquad
    \mu_{\ln} = \ln(\mathrm{mean}) - \frac{\sigma_{\ln}^2}{2},
    \label{eq:lognormal_params}
\end{equation}
the second term in $\mu_{\ln}$ ensuring $E[X] = \mathrm{mean}$ exactly. The
distance between the calibrated mean and the independently bisected escape
threshold, expressed in standard deviations of the underlying normal
variable $\ln X$, is
\begin{equation}
    z = \frac{\ln(\text{threshold}) - \mu_{\ln}}{\sigma_{\ln}},
    \label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
and the predicted escape fraction is the tail probability of the standard
normal cumulative distribution function (CDF),
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(z) = \frac{1}{2}\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right],
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
evaluated on whichever side of $z$ corresponds to escape for that
parameter. Which side that is follows directly from the pharmacology, not
from a free choice: a \emph{higher} sampled $EC_{50,L}$ means a
\emph{weaker} drug (more concentration needed for the same effect), so
escape is the \emph{upper} tail, $P(Z>z) = 1-\Phi(z)$; a \emph{lower}
sampled $E_{max,L}$ or $k_{immune}$ means weaker killing capacity or weaker
immune clearance respectively, so for both, escape is the \emph{lower}
tail, $P(Z<z) = \Phi(z)$. Because the threshold in each case is located
independently by bisection on the mechanistic model
(\Cref{sec:s2,sec:s3,sec:s6}) rather than fit to the sampling distribution,
\Cref{eq:zscore} is not circular: it is an after-the-fact check of how far
apart two independently-derived numbers happen to sit, not a definition of
either one.

As a worked example, $EC_{50,L}$'s calibration (mean $=0.6$~mg/L,
$\mathrm{CV}=1.1$, threshold $=0.868$~mg/L; \Cref{sec:s2}) gives, from
\Cref{eq:lognormal_params}, $\sigma_{\ln} = \sqrt{\ln(1+1.1^2)} = 0.8905$
and $\mu_{\ln} = \ln(0.6) - 0.8905^2/2 = -0.9073$; substituting into
\Cref{eq:zscore} gives $z = \left[\ln(0.868) - (-0.9073)\right]/0.8905
\approx 0.860$. Since escape is the upper tail for $EC_{50,L}$, the
predicted escape fraction from \Cref{eq:phi} is $1-\Phi(0.860) =
1-0.8051 = 0.1949$, i.e.\ 19.5\% --- the value reported in the first row of
\Cref{tab:zscore_summary}. The other two rows follow the identical
procedure, substituting each parameter's own mean, CV, and threshold and
taking the lower tail instead of the upper. In practice $\Phi$ is evaluated
numerically (e.g.\ \texttt{scipy.stats.norm.cdf(z)}) rather than by table
lookup, but \Cref{eq:phi} is the exact quantity being computed.

\Cref{tab:zscore_summary} applies \Cref{eq:lognormal_params,eq:zscore} to
the three calibrated sweeps. The $z$-score alone, with no other information
about the underlying ODE model, predicts each reported escape fraction to
within a few percentage points. In every case the calibrated mean sits
under one standard deviation from its own threshold, though $EC_{50,L}$
now sits somewhat further out (0.86~SD) than $E_{max,L}$ and $k_{immune}$
(0.67--0.70~SD) --- a deliberate consequence of lowering $EC_{50,L}$'s
calibrated mean to make linezolid more potent at sub-saturating
concentrations (\Cref{sec:s2}), not an inconsistency in the underlying
relationship.

Because the mean-to-threshold gap is small in all three cases, the escape
fractions themselves carry less information than they first appear to:
each is largely a readout of how close its calibration landed to its own
threshold, not an independent measurement of clinical risk. The finding
worth stating plainly is therefore the gap itself, not the exact
percentages it produces: the calibrated model is not comfortably clear of
relapse along any of the three dimensions examined --- a drug-potency
parameter, a drug-efficacy parameter, and a host-immunity parameter,
calibrated separately from one another --- it sits close to criticality in
all three, independently and simultaneously, with $EC_{50,L}$ now the
furthest (but still sub-1-SD) of the three by design. Whether that
reflects an incidental property of this particular calibration, or a more
general feature of relapsing reservoir infections that have already
reached the point of requiring salvage linezolid therapy, is a question
this model cannot resolve on its own; the consistency across three
parameters that were neither designed nor fit together is at least
suggestive of the latter.

\section{Scalar outcome sweeps for the compartment exchange rates}
\label{sec:s7}

\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes} summarizes Sweep~1 ($f_{r \to b}$ varied,
$f_{b \to r}$ held at baseline) in a single view: peak $S_b$, peak $R_b$,
and final $R_b$ overlaid on one log-scale axis, with the swept range shaded
by regime and the two precise, bisected thresholds marked. The two
thresholds answer different clinical questions and sit at opposite ends of
the six-decade range. The establishment threshold
($f_{r \to b} = 2.44\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$) asks whether bacteremia
happens at all: below it, the reservoir-to-blood seeding flux is too small
for blood to ever accumulate enough bacteria to escape the model's
near-zero floor, so infection simply never establishes over the entire
81-day course. The clearance threshold
($f_{r \to b} = 8.06\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), roughly 330-fold
higher, asks a different question given that bacteremia does establish:
does it relapse after treatment, or resolve permanently? Below it ---
across the whole plateau in between --- resistant bacteremia establishes,
is suppressed by linezolid during therapy, and relapses afterward because
the reservoir sanctuary is never fully sterilized. Above it, transfer
becomes fast and one-directional enough that the reservoir stops
protecting the resistant strain: bacteria are funneled into blood faster
than they return, where they face far stronger immune killing and
linezolid's full, undiscounted effect, driving both compartments to
extinction together and leaving nothing behind to cause a relapse. In
short, the low threshold is about ignition --- can the reservoir seed
blood at all --- while the high threshold is about sanctuary --- can the
reservoir protect the resistant strain long enough to cause relapse; the
broad plateau between them is where the exact rate barely matters because
the outcome is already decided by which side of each threshold it falls
on.

Concretely, below the establishment threshold (bisected on the point where
final $R_b$ crosses the LOD) all three curves sit at or near zero, then
switch sharply to their full baseline levels within roughly half a
log-decade of the model's own baseline rate ($5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$).
This transition is a threshold across independent simulated courses ---
each grid point holds its own $f_{r \to b}$ fixed for the entire simulated
course --- rather than a switch occurring partway through any single
patient's timeline. Peak $S_{res}$ (the light dotted reference line in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes}) varies by less than 6\% across the full
six-decade range and is effectively flat on the scale of the figure.

Across the broad plateau separating the two transitions ($2.44\times10^{-5}$
to $8.06\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, the salmon-shaded band in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes}), blood and reservoir diverge: once
established, $R_b$ is suppressed below the LOD for essentially the entire
linezolid course (at the baseline rate, $R_b$ falls from a pre-linezolid peak
of $\sim4\times10^3$~CFU/mL to $\sim0.04$~CFU/mL within days of linezolid
starting and stays there until the course ends), while $R_{res}$ is only
partially suppressed and never crosses the LOD, settling on a lower plateau
(down from a pre-treatment peak of $\sim9\times10^3$~CFU/mL) because
linezolid reaches the reservoir at only
$\mathrm{lzd\_res\_fraction} = 0.30$ of its blood exposure. That suppressed
level is not itself constant across the band: sampled at 40 log-spaced rates
strictly inside the plateau, $R_{res}$ at the end of the linezolid course
falls monotonically with $f_{r \to b}$, from $\sim5.9\times10^2$~CFU/mL just
above the establishment threshold ($\sim5.9\times10^2$ at the baseline rate
of $5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$) to $\sim4.5\times10^1$~CFU/mL just below
the clearance threshold --- a factor of 13 across the band, and within a
factor of $\sim4.5$ of the LOD at its upper end. The reservoir is therefore
progressively drained as $f_{r \to b}$ rises, well before the clearance
threshold is reached; the sanctuary weakens continuously rather than
surviving intact until it fails abruptly. Once linezolid is
withdrawn, this uncleared reservoir reseeds blood through $f_{r \to b}$, and
$R_b$ rebounds within days to a level comparable to its pre-treatment peak.
Final $R_b$ therefore closely tracks peak $R_b$ not because the resistant
population sits at carrying capacity in blood throughout treatment, but
because the post-treatment relapse reaches the same order of magnitude as the
pre-treatment escape: linezolid clears the resistant strain from blood but
never eradicates it from its reservoir sanctuary, for essentially the entire
escape range.

\Cref{tab:frb_plateau_relapse} makes this within-course sequence explicit at
seven rates spanning the plateau. Two features are worth noting. First, the
relapse is complete rather than partial: final $R_b$ matches peak $R_b$ to
three significant figures at every rate sampled, so the post-treatment
rebound recovers the full pre-treatment burden and not some fraction of it.
Second, the relapse is strictly a withdrawal phenomenon --- $R_b$ stays
between $\sim10^{-2}$ and $\sim1.4$~CFU/mL for the entire 42-day linezolid
course, two to three orders of magnitude below the LOD, and only crosses
back above it 0.5--2.9~days \textit{after} the course ends. Nothing
resembling relapse occurs while drug is on board. The rebound lag shortens
as $f_{r \to b}$ increases, for the same reason $R_{res}$ at end-of-course
falls: faster reservoir-to-blood transfer both drains the sanctuary during
treatment and reseeds blood more rapidly once treatment stops.

A second, smaller-magnitude trend operates within the same plateau: peak
$S_b$ --- which occurs pre-vancomycin, around day 21, regardless of
$f_{r \to b}$ --- declines gradually and monotonically as $f_{r \to b}$
increases across the plateau (from $\sim4.82\times10^3$~CFU/mL near the
establishment threshold to $\sim4.25$--$4.39\times10^3$~CFU/mL near the
upper transition, roughly a 12\% decline). Both strains occupy blood
simultaneously during the pre-vancomycin window and share its logistic
carrying capacity ($B_{max,blood} = 6000$~CFU/mL); a
higher $f_{r \to b}$ also seeds more resistant bacteria into blood earlier
($R_b$ at day 21 rises from $\sim0.8$ to $\sim720$~CFU/mL across the same
range), so the growing co-resident $R_b$ population increasingly crowds
$S_b$ out of its own pre-treatment growth ceiling. This is distinct from,
and much smaller in magnitude than, both the establishment threshold and
the post-treatment relapse dynamics described above: it does not reflect
$S_b$ being displaced from the reservoir (peak $S_{res}$ stays essentially
flat across this same range, as already noted, since $S_{res}$ and
$R_{res}$ do not compete for reservoir carrying capacity), only from the
shared blood compartment the two strains briefly co-occupy before
vancomycin clears $S_b$.

At the upper end of the range (the mediumseagreen-shaded band in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes}), final $R_b$ undergoes a second, independent
transition, dropping back to zero for $f_{r \to b} \gtrsim
8.06\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ even as peak $R_b$ continues to rise ---
$R_b$ still establishes and peaks at these rates, but no longer relapses by
the end of follow-up. Unlike the plateau regime, this second transition
\textit{is} a genuine within-course dynamic: $R_b$ rises to near carrying
capacity during the outbreak and is then driven back down by linezolid and
the immune response before follow-up ends --- and $R_{res}$ collapses along
with it. Because $f_{b \to r}$ is held at its baseline value while $f_{r \to
b}$ climbs a further two decades, transfer becomes strongly
one-directional: reservoir bacteria are funneled into blood, where they are
exposed to immune killing an order of magnitude stronger than in the
reservoir, and to linezolid's full, undiscounted effect (rather than the
reservoir's tissue-penetration-scaled fraction), faster than they return. At
sufficiently high $f_{r \to b}$, the reservoir therefore stops acting as a
protected sanctuary and instead feeds the resistant population into a
compartment where it is cleared, driving both $R_b$ and $R_{res}$ to
extinction together.

\section{Sampling grid for the compartment exchange-rate sweeps}
\label{sec:s5}

The two exchange-rate sweeps in \Cref{sec:s7}
($f_{r \to b}$, reservoir $\to$ blood; $f_{b \to r}$, blood $\to$ reservoir)
each step their target rate across a shared log-spaced grid spanning six
decades ($10^{-8}$--$10^{-2}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$), while holding the other
exchange rate fixed at its own baseline value
($f_{r \to b} = 5\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$,
$f_{b \to r} = 1\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$). $f_{r \to b}$'s transition
band, where established resistant bacteremia switches from relapsing to
clearing outright, falls within $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$
(\Cref{sec:s7}); $f_{b \to r}$ produces no comparable switch anywhere in the
swept range, but its steepest response --- the monotonic rise in peak
$S_{res}$ --- is also concentrated in that same band. The grid is therefore
deliberately non-uniform for both sweeps: a coarse 40-point log-spaced pass
covers the full six-decade range for context, supplemented by a dense
160-point pass restricted to $[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, so that
$f_{r \to b}$'s sharp switch and the steepest portion of $f_{b \to r}$'s
smooth $S_{res}$ response (both visible in
\Cref{fig:supp_frb_regimes,fig:supp_fbr_regimes}) are both resolved rather
than aliased.
\Cref{fig:transfer_rate_grid} shows the resulting grid coverage for both
sweeps directly, with each sweep's own baseline rate marked.

    \caption{Log-spaced grid coverage used for the two exchange-rate sweeps

\section{Deterministic escape threshold in initial reservoir burden ($S_{res,0}$)}
\label{sec:s3}

\Cref{fig:sres0_threshold_sweep} sweeps the initial reservoir sensitive-strain
load $S_{res,0} \in [1, 2000]~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$ (101 points), holding
$R_{res,0} = 100~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$ and every other parameter at baseline, to
test how sensitive the escape outcome is to the size of the pre-treatment
reservoir population rather than to any pharmacodynamic parameter. Unlike the
$EC_{50,L}$ sweep in \Cref{sec:s2}, no clearance threshold exists anywhere in
the swept range: both $R_b$ and $R_{res}$ finish above the LOD at every one
of the 101 tested $S_{res,0}$ values, from 1 to 2000~CFU/mL. The model's own
default, $S_{res,0} = 100~\mathrm{CFU/mL}$, is marked directly on the panel
for reference, but is not distinguished from any other tested value in its
outcome. Resistant escape in this model is therefore not an artifact of the
specific initial reservoir burden assumed in the main text --- it persists
regardless of where $S_{res,0}$ is set within a two-decade range around that
default.
