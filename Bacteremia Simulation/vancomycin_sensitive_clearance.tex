\section{Summary}

Under baseline parameters, the 4-day vancomycin course
(\texttt{van\_duration} $= 96$ h, starting at \texttt{vanco\_start} $=
504$ h) drives the vancomycin-sensitive strain to functional extinction in
both compartments -- blood ($S_b$) and reservoir ($S_{res}$) -- before
linezolid is ever started. Vancomycin monotherapy is therefore sufficient,
in this model, to clear the sensitive strain; it does not require the
subsequent linezolid course. With $E_{max,V}$ raised from $0.40$ to $1.7$
h$^{-1}$ (literature-consistent value; $EC_{50,V}$ also raised slightly,
from $0.245$ to $0.25$ mg/L), this clearance is now essentially complete
within the first day of the course rather than unfolding gradually across
all four days.

\section{Mechanism}

Vancomycin acts only on $S_b$ and $S_{res}$ (the model gives resistant
bacteria, $R_b$/$R_{res}$, no vancomycin susceptibility term at all). Its
effect enters as a saturating $E_{max}$ term,
\begin{equation}
E_V(t) = \frac{E_{max,V}\, V(t)}{EC_{50,V} + V(t)},
\end{equation}
with $E_{max,V} = 1.7$ h$^{-1}$ and $EC_{50,V} = 0.25$ mg/L. Because
q8h dosing (1000 mg, $V_d = 50$ L, $f_u = 0.50$) keeps free drug well above
$EC_{50,V}$ for nearly the whole interval, $E_V(t)$ sits close to its
ceiling $E_{max,V}$ for most of the 96-h course rather than fluctuating
with the dosing troughs.

In the blood compartment, the net rate governing $S_b$ during the
vancomycin window (before linezolid starts, so $E_L = 0$) is
\begin{equation}
\dot S_b \approx \Big[\rho_S \cdot \underbrace{\big(1 - \tfrac{S_b+R_b}{B_{max,blood}}\big)}_{\text{logistic}} - k_{immune}\,\mathrm{eff}_{blood} - E_V(t)\Big] S_b ,
\end{equation}
with $\rho_S = 0.60$ h$^{-1}$, $k_{immune}\,\mathrm{eff}_{blood} = 0.12$
h$^{-1}$, and $E_V \to E_{max,V} = 1.7$ h$^{-1}$ at saturating
concentrations. Once $S_b$ is small relative to $B_{max,blood} = 6000$
CFU/mL, the logistic term is $\approx 1$ and the bracketed net rate is
$0.60 - 0.12 - 1.7 = -1.22$ h$^{-1}$ -- i.e.\ vancomycin's direct kill now
dominates blood-compartment growth outright, so $S_b$ declines from the
moment vancomycin saturates rather than depending on the reservoir to
collapse first. $S_{res}$ is subject to the same vancomycin effect, scaled
by tissue penetration (\texttt{van\_res\_fraction} $= 0.15$), plus immune
killing at $\mathrm{eff}_{res} = 0.1$, and clears on an essentially
identical timescale, so the reseeding term $f_{r\to b}\,S_{res}$ that feeds
$S_b$ collapses in parallel rather than as a prerequisite; the smooth
low-density floor built into the growth term ($S_b^2/(10^2+S_b^2)$)
suppresses any residual net growth as $S_b$ itself approaches zero, so both
compartments now run down together almost immediately, rather than $S_b$
lagging behind a reservoir-driven collapse as under the old $E_{max,V} =
0.40$ h$^{-1}$ (where the direct blood-compartment rate was still slightly
positive, $+0.08$ h$^{-1}$).

By $+24$ h into the course, $S_b$ and $S_{res}$ have already fallen below
$10^{-11}$ CFU/mL -- many orders of magnitude below any realistic limit of
detection (LOD $=10$ CFU/mL is used elsewhere in this project) -- and
continue to decay toward numerical zero for the rest of the course, so the
sensitive strain is cleared in every practical sense within the first day
of treatment. Unlike the old $E_{max,V} = 0.40$ h$^{-1}$ trajectory, where
the decline was gradual and back-loaded (still $9.90\times10^{2}$ CFU/mL at
$+24$ h, not reaching near-zero until the final day of the course), the
decline here is sharp and front-loaded: vancomycin's own direct kill in
blood (Section 2) is now strong enough on its own to drive $S_b$ down
immediately, with the reservoir clearing in parallel rather than as a
precondition.

\section{Competitive release of the resistant strain}

Vancomycin has no effect on $R_b$ (\texttt{dual\_reservoir\_model} gives
$R_b$ no vancomycin-kill term at all -- it is susceptible only to
linezolid). As $S_b$ is cleared, blood carrying capacity
($B_{max,blood} = 6000$ CFU/mL) that was previously occupied by the
sensitive population is vacated, and $R_b$ expands into it: over the same
96-h window $R_b$ rises from $2.10$ to $4.42\times10^{3}$ CFU/mL (Table
\ref{tab:sb}). In this model, vancomycin's complete clearance of the
sensitive strain is therefore the mechanism that permits the resistant
strain's emergence in blood, rather than a side effect independent of it.
With the faster $E_{max,V} = 1.7$ h$^{-1}$ clearance, this competitive
release is also compressed in time: $R_b$ already reaches $92\%$ of its
final level by $+48$ h (vs.\ only $\sim 38\%$ under the old $E_{max,V} =
0.40$ h$^{-1}$ trajectory), because $S_b$ vacates the shared carrying
capacity within the first day rather than the last. The end-of-course
value is essentially unchanged from the old baseline, however -- by the
time the vancomycin course ends the two trajectories have already
reconverged, so nothing downstream (the subsequent linezolid course, or
final outcomes) is affected by how quickly this release happens.
